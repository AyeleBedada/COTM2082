Course Introduction

This course is designed to provide practical knowledge and hands-on experience in construction drafting, working drawing preparation, multidisciplinary coordination, and technical research within the built environment.

1. Construction Drafting

Students will develop practical skills in construction drafting by learning how to incorporate drawing conventions, building codes, standards, specifications, and construction requirements into technical drawings.
The course introduces the major components of a building, including both the substructure and superstructure. Students will learn how to accurately represent and specify these building elements in accordance with standard construction documentation practices.
In addition, students will gain an understanding of how various construction materials and construction techniques are integrated into building drawings. While future courses will focus on the detailed application of individual materials and construction systems through specialized projects, this course emphasizes buildings that incorporate multiple materials and construction methods within a single project. Based on the assigned project, students will investigate the construction materials used and the techniques required for their implementation.

2. Working Drawings and Construction Documentation

Students will learn to prepare a complete set of working drawings that provide detailed information necessary for the construction of a building. These drawings will extend beyond general architectural representations and will serve as comprehensive construction documents.
The documentation package is expected to include:
• Detailed architectural drawings
• Construction details
• Schedules
• General notes
• Specific notes
• Other supporting documentation required for construction
Students will also learn proper drawing management practices, including numbering, indexing, and version control, to facilitate efficient organization, retrieval, and revision of project documents.

3. Multidisciplinary Drawing Coordination

A key component of the course is the coordination of drawings and documents produced by different disciplines involved in the built environment. Students will learn to identify and resolve conflicts among drawings prepared by:
• Architectural disciplines
• Sanitary and plumbing services
• Electrical systems
• HVAC systems
• Mechanical services
Furthermore, students will develop skills in coordinating drawings with relevant schedules, codes, standards, specifications, and other project documentation to ensure consistency and constructability.

4. Seminar and Technical Research

Students will participate in group-based seminars focused on innovative and contemporary topics related to construction technology and the built environment. Possible topics may include construction techniques, building codes, standards, specifications, project documentation, or coordination methodologies.
Each group will research an assigned or selected topic, critically analyze relevant information, and present their findings to the class. The seminar component is intended to strengthen students' research, communication, and presentation skills while fostering peer-to-peer learning.
